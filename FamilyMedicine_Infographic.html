Overview

Marriage is the fundamental building block of society, and the cornerstone in building emotional, healthy and family relationships. The healthy marriage guarantees protecting the family members from the hereditary and infectious diseases; thus building a happy and stable family.

DEFINITION
Premarital care is the specific service provided for the couples before marriage. By caring for the health of present children and adolescents, we are actually starting to provide care for the future generation.
TIMING
Couples planning to marry are advised to conduct premarital screening at least three months before the marriage date. The premarital compatibility certificate is valid for only six months.

Premarital Counselling

DEFINITION
Counselling is an interpersonal communication designed to help another person to make a rationale decision. Premarital counseling has been defined as a process of helping couple learn how to make wise and realistic decisions, through providing information and altering their own behaviors to produce desirable consequences.

Process of Counselling:

👂 Listening — be accepting, concerned, patient, specific, honest and faithful
💬 Discussion
🗣️ Talking & Negotiation — Explaining & Education
Types of Premarital Counselling
I — General Counselling & Health Education
Promoting healthy lifestyle, protection against preventable disease and unhealthy behaviors such as smoking, addiction.
II — Reproductive Health Counselling
Advice about appropriate time of 1st pregnancy, importance of early antenatal care, precautions during pregnancy (avoid drugs & teratogenic hazards), safe delivery.
III — Relevant Health Problem Counselling
Management of cases and referral to specialists for any discovered problem during premarital assessment.
IV — Genetic Counselling
Studying genogram of both parents to discover probability of genetic hazards to offspring (SCD, thalassemia, Marfan syndrome, deaf mutism). Referral for genetic testing when indicated.

Inter-conception Care

DEFINITION
Caring for females in-between pregnancies aimed to:
  1. Promote the health and nutritional status of the mother
  2. Identify risks both for the mother and the future children and deal with them
  3. Decrease maternal morbidity and mortality
  4. Decrease neonatal morbidity and mortality

During Post partum / post abortive care
When attending the MCH for her child care
During home visits

I — Counselling
  1. Contraceptive counselling — help females choose most appropriate method
  2. Nutritional counselling
  3. Genetic counselling — when history of recurrent abortion, cervical incompetence, congenital abnormalities

General health education and promotion

  • Recently diagnosed maternal diseases (DM, HTN)
  • Obstetric history: previous complicated pregnancy, bad outcome (Down, β-thalassaemia, SCD, NTD, congenital hypothyroidism)

Definition & Goal of Antenatal Care

DEFINITION
Antenatal care (ANC) is an umbrella term used to describe the medical procedures and care that are carried out during pregnancy. It is the care a woman receives throughout her pregnancy and is important in helping to ensure a healthy pregnancy state and safe childbirth.

Goal of Antenatal Care

🎯 Ultimate Goal
Reduce maternal and perinatal mortality and morbidity rates.
Prepare the woman for labor, lactation, and care of her infant. Detect early and treat properly complicated conditions that could endanger the life or impair the health of the mother or the fetus.
How This is Achieved
  1. Detection & management of complications (medical, surgical, obstetrical)
  2. Promote physical, mental, social health of mother & baby — nutrition, hygiene, birthing process
  3. Develop birth preparedness & complication readiness plan
  4. Prepare mother to breastfeed successfully, experience normal puerperium, and care for child

First Antenatal Care Visit

A. History Taking
1. LMP & EDD
Date of last menstrual period to calculate expected date of delivery by Naegele's rule (subtract 3 months, add 7 days to 1st day of LMP). For irregular cycles, EDD by US before 24 weeks.
2. Current Problems
Nausea & vomiting, abdominal pain, headache, urinary complaints, vaginal bleeding, edema, backache, heartburn, constipation.
3. Past Medical & Surgical
Chronic diseases (DM, HTN, renal), prior operations (CS, genital repair), blood transfusion, accidents involving bony pelvis, allergies, medications.
4. Gynecological History
Menstrual history, contraceptive history, history of STDs.
5-6. Medications & Nutrition
Current prescriptions & OTC, substance use (alcohol, tobacco), nutritional history.
7-8. Family & Obstetric History
Family: DM, HTN, heart disease, multiple pregnancy, hereditary diseases.
Obstetric: gravida, para, abortion, living children, previous complications, type of delivery, birth weights.
9-10. Genetic & Social History
Genetic: hereditary diseases.
Social: Was pregnancy planned/unwanted? Screen for intimate partner violence — increases risk of spontaneous abortion, placental abruption, PROM, LBW, prematurity.
B. Physical Examination
General Examination
  • Blood pressure (baseline)
  • Thyroid, breast exam
  • Height & body weight (baseline for BMI)
  • Edema evaluation
Local Examination
  • Fundal level assessment (after 12 wks)
  • Auscultation fetal heart tones: Doppler 10-12 wks, Pinard's stethoscope after 20 wks
Ultrasound
  • Estimate gestational age
  • Check amniotic fluid volume
  • Placental position
  • Detect multiple pregnancy & congenital malformations
  • Fetal position
Dating accuracy: ±4-7 days (1st tri), ±10-14 days (2nd tri), ±21 days (3rd tri).
Standard: offer single US at 18-20 wks for anatomic screening.

Repeated ANC Visits

Each subsequent visit includes the following components (a–j):

a) Update History
Update the patient's history for any new complaints.
b) Evaluate Symptoms
Evaluate symptoms suggestive of complications — gestational hypertension, infections (urinary tract, vaginal), placenta previa: headache, blurring of vision, vaginal bleeding, abdominal pain, dysuria, vaginal discharge, and subjective sensation of fetal movement.
c) Assess Weight Gain
At 20th week ~3.5 kg gained, then ~0.5 kg/week. Total: 11–16 kg.
⚠ Excessive gain → occult edema of pre-eclampsia
⚠ Failure to gain → fetal jeopardy
d) Assess Blood Pressure
BP measurement at each visit to detect pregnancy-induced hypertension.
e) Assess Fundal Height
Measurement of fundal height is recommended at each prenatal visit beginning at 20 weeks.
f) Auscultate Fetal Heart Rate
Recommended at each prenatal visit to confirm a viable fetus.
g) Evaluate Edema
Evaluate edema of the extremities or face — may indicate pregnancy-induced hypertension.
h) Leopold Maneuvers
Abdominal palpation to assess fetal presentation beginning at 36 weeks' gestation (less accurate earlier in pregnancy).
Investigations at Each Visit
i) Urine Analysis
Recommended at each visit for protein, glucose, or infection.
j) Screening Tests During Pregnancy
  1. Trisomy & NTD screening (15–20 wks): Triple screen (HCG, unconjugated estriol, α-fetoprotein) or quadruple screen (+ inhibin-A). Nuchal translucency by US.
  2. GDM screening (24–28 wks): 1-hour 50g glucose challenge test. Value >140 mg/dl → abnormal. Positive → 3-hour GTT (100g glucose, fasting + 1h + 2h + 3h). Two of four positive = gestational diabetes.

Detection & Management of High-Risk Pregnancy

IMPORTANT
Risky pregnancy can be detected from history and first antenatal examination or may develop during pregnancy. Early detection is important to prevent complications and hazards.
I — Risk Factors Detected from History
Mother's age <18 or >35 years
Primigravida or grand multipara (5th pregnancy)
Birth spacing <2 years
Previous fetal death / repeated abortions
History of Caesarean section
Previous baby with congenital anomalies
Chronic diseases (HTN, DM, renal, heart)
II — Risk Factors Detected Early in Pregnancy
Diabetes mellitus
Severe anemia
Severe persistent vomiting
Severe undernutrition
III — Risk Factors Detected Late in Pregnancy
Severe PIH
Antepartum hemorrhage
Multiple pregnancy
IUGR
Abnormal presentation
MANAGEMENT
High risk pregnancy should be observed more closely and referred to specialist when needed. Mandatory hospital delivery.

MOST COMMON

Curative Management of Iron Deficiency Anemia
  • Give ferrous fumarate or sulphate 200 mg 3 times daily

Used for most pregnant women with iron deficiency who can tolerate it. Used for all individuals in the 1st trimester.
IV Iron — Indications
  • Beyond 1st trimester & cannot tolerate oral iron
  • Severe or symptomatic anemia (especially later in pregnancy)
  • Oral iron ineffective (impaired absorption/adherence)

3 Physiological Methods

Note: These methods have a high failure rate and are best used with backup methods.

The female identifies the fertile time of her menstrual cycle and avoids unprotected intercourse during that period.

Calendar-Based Methods

Standard Days Method: Avoid unprotected intercourse on days 8 through 19 of regular menstrual cycles.

Symptoms-Based Methods

Observing signs of ovulation:

  • Cervical secretions: Increase and become thin
  • Basal Body Temperature (BBT): Rises by 0.5°C after ovulation

Pouch inserted into the vagina. Provides dual protection.

Foam, gel, cream, film, suppository, or tablet. Placed in vagina ≤1 hr before intercourse, left in place ≥6-8 hrs after.

Special Situations

Managing Missed Pills

Key message: Take a missed pill ASAP. Keep taking one pill daily — 2 pills can be taken the same day.

Missed 1–2 Pills

Take the most recent pill ASAP, continue the rest as usual.

Missed ≥3 Pills (Weeks 1–2)

Take most recent pill ASAP. Use backup method for next 7 days.

Missed ≥3 Pills (Week 3)

2. Implants

  • Small plastic rods placed in subcutaneous space of upper arm
  • Provide long-term protection for 3–5 years (depends on type)
  • Immediately reversible upon removal
  • Mechanism: Prevents ovulation + thickens cervical mucus
  • Effectiveness: Far less than 1 pregnancy/100 women/year
Side Effects
  • Similar to progestin-only injectable, but:
  • No effect on bone density
  • No delay in regaining fertility after removal
  • More changes in bleeding patterns
  • Infection at insertion site
  • Difficult removal (rare if properly inserted by skilled provider)

9 Intrauterine Devices (IUDs)

1. Copper-Bearing IUD (Cu-IUD)

T-shaped device wrapped with copper wire. Works by inducing inflammatory reaction in endometrium, hostile to sperm motility — prevents fertilization and implantation.

ParameterDetails
Effectiveness<1 pregnancy/100 women/year over first year; ~2/100 over 10 years
DurationTCu-380A effective for 10 years
Return of fertilityNo delay after removal
Side EffectsMenorrhagia, irregular bleeding (first 3–6 months), dysmenorrhea
Complications
  • Menorrhagia may contribute to anemia if iron stores are low
  • Infections: PID, tubo-ovarian abscesses
  • Rare: Perforation (usually heals without treatment)
  • Rare: Miscarriage/preterm birth/infection if pregnancy occurs with IUD in place
  • Difficult removal
  • Unrecognized expulsion → unintended pregnancy
Contraindications
  • Between 48 hours and 4 weeks postpartum
  • Puerperal sepsis or septic abortion (re-evaluate after treatment)
  • Unexplained vaginal bleeding suggesting pregnancy or underlying condition
  • Current cervical, endometrial, or ovarian cancer
  • High risk for STIs (unless gonorrhea/chlamydia ruled out)
  • History of ectopic pregnancy (not advisable — if IUD fails, 6–8% of pregnancies are ectopic)
Special Situations
Heavy / Prolonged Bleeding

Reassure: common, usually resolves after first few months.

Management (try one at a time):

  • Tranexamic acid 1500 mg TID × 3 days, then 1000 mg daily × 2 days
  • NSAIDs (ibuprofen 400 mg or indomethacin 25 mg) BID × 5 days — avoid aspirin
  • Iron supplements + iron-rich foods
  • If persists → consider removal
Cramping Pain

Expected first 1–2 days after insertion. Also common in first 3–6 months during menstruation. Decreases over time.

Ibuprofen 200–400 mg or paracetamol 325–1000 mg.

If severe cramping beyond 2 days → evaluate for partial expulsion or perforation.

Missing Strings

Ask: Saw IUD come out? Last menstruation? Pregnancy symptoms? Backup method used?

Check cervical canal with forceps (~50% found here). If not found → refer for ultrasound (or X-ray if pregnancy excluded).

2. Levonorgestrel IUD (LNG-IUD / Hormonal IUD)

T-shaped plastic device that steadily releases levonorgestrel. Works by preventing fertilization — profound change in cervical mucin making it impenetrable by sperm. Local effect on endometrium diminishes menstrual bleeding.

ParameterDetails
Effectiveness<1 pregnancy/100 women/year
DurationMirena & Kyleena — up to 5 years
Return of fertilityNo delay after removal
Non-Contraceptive Advantages
  • Helps protect against: Iron-deficiency anemia
  • May help protect against: Endometrial cancer, cervical cancer
  • Reduces: Dysmenorrhea, heavy menstrual bleeding, endometriosis symptoms
Side Effects
  • Bleeding pattern changes (lighter, fewer days, infrequent, irregular, or absent; prolonged — least common)
  • Acne, headaches, breast tenderness, nausea, weight gain, dizziness, mood changes
Contraindications
  • Between 48 hours and 4 weeks since giving birth
  • History of DVT or pulmonary embolism
  • History of breast cancer
  • Liver cirrhosis or tumor
  • Current ovarian or endometrial cancer
  • High risk for STIs at insertion
