1. Abnormal Uterine Action

Atonic Uterus — Wikimedia Commons (CC)

Definition

Inefficient, weak and/or inordinate uterine contractions which fail to cause progressive cervical dilatation and effacement. Abnormal uterine action is one of the 3 "P" s of dystocia: Power (contractions), Passages (pelvis), Passenger (fetus).

3
Frequency (1 in 3 min)
45
Duration (45 sec)
60
Amplitude (60 mmHg)

Classification

✅ Coordinate (normal polarity)
  • Hypertonic — precipitate labor
  • Tonic contraction — Bandl's ring
  • Hypotonic — hypotonic inertia
❌ Incoordinate (abnormal polarity)
  • Spastic lower segment
  • Colicky uterus
  • Constriction ring
  • Generalized tonic contraction
  • Cervical dystocia

Excessive Uterine Action — Definitions

TermDefinition
Uterine tachysystole4 or more contractions in 10 minutes
Uterine hyperstimulationTachysystole + occurs with oxytocin + FHR abnormality
Tetanic contractionSingle contraction sustained for > 3 minutes
Hypertonic contractionElevated basal tone > 20 mmHg

First-Aid for Uterine Overactivity (5 Steps)

  1. Lateral position — relieves aortocaval compression
  2. Oxygenation — supplemental O₂
  3. Discontinue oxytocin — most common reversible cause
  4. Tocolytic agent — terbutaline or MgSO₄
  5. Consider immediate delivery — if persistently ominous FHR

Precipitate Labor

Labor lasting less than 3 hours. More common in multiparas with strong contractions, small baby, and roomy pelvis.

🤰 Maternal Complications
  • Lacerations of cervix, vagina, perineum
  • Placental abruption
  • Shock
  • Inversion of the uterus
  • PPH (no time for retraction)
  • Sepsis
👶 Fetal Complications
  • Intracranial hemorrhage
  • Fetal asphyxia
  • Avulsion of umbilical cord
  • Fetal injury from falling

Bandl's Ring (Pathological Retraction Ring)

A groove between symphysis and umbilicus during obstructed labor due to marked retraction and thickening of the upper segment while the lower segment is markedly stretched and thinned. Sign of impending uterine rupture — obstetric emergency!

Hypotonic Inertia

Infrequent weak uterine contractions. Predisposing factors: elderly primigravida, anemia, anxiety, improper analgesics, malpresentations, overdistended uterus.

📋 Management of Hypotonic Inertia
General: Exclude disproportion, encourage ambulation/hydration. Amniotomy if cervix >3 cm and head engaged. Oxytocin augmentation — start 2 miu/min, increase until 3 contractions/10 min. Delivery: Vaginal with instrumental aid if cervix fully dilated; CS if failure.

Hypertonic Inertia

Reversed polarity — fundal dominance lacking. Types: spastic lower segment, colicky uterus. Irregular painful contractions with no relaxation. Management: obstetric analgesia (pethidine, epidural); CS if CPD or fetal distress.

Constriction Ring

Persistent localized annular spasm at any part of uterus, usually at junction of upper and lower segments. Felt only vaginally (unlike Bandl's ring which is seen abdominally). Management: tocolytic or general anesthesia; if relaxed → forceps; if not → CS.

💡 Key Difference
Bandl's ring = rising between symphysis & umbilicus, seen/felt abdominally, sign of impending rupture. Constriction ring = at any level, felt only vaginally, may be relaxed by anesthesia.

2. Obstructed Labor

Shoulder Dystocia — Wikimedia Commons (CC)

Definition

⚠️ Key Definition
Failure of delivery of fetus in spite of good uterine contractions due to mechanical obstruction. Vaginal delivery is contraindicated — cesarean section is the only safe route.

Causes

🤰 Maternal (Passages)
  • Contracted pelvis
  • Abnormal pelvis (android/anthropoid)
  • Pelvic tumor (fibroid, ovarian tumor)
  • Tumors of rectum, bladder, pelvic bone
  • Cervical stenosis
  • Vaginal septum
  • Constricting ring
👶 Fetal (Passenger)
  • Macrosomic baby
  • Malpresentation
  • Malposition
  • Malformed fetus (hydrocephalos, conjoint twins, fetal ascitis)
  • Locked twins

Diagnosis — The Triad

A. General Examination

  • Labor prolonged > 12 hours
  • Mother exhausted, anxious, weak
  • Maternal distress: dehydration, ketoacidosis, tachypnea, scanty urine

B. Abdominal Examination

  • Uterus hard and tender
  • Pathological retraction ring (Bandl's ring)
  • Distended bladder
  • Fetal distress or absent FHS

C. Vaginal Examination

  • Vulva edematous, vagina dry and hot
  • Cervix fully/partially dilated and edematous
  • Membranes ruptured
  • Presenting part high, not engaged — large caput and moulding
  • Cause of obstruction detected

Management

Prevention

  1. Proper ANC assessment
  2. Early detection of maternal/fetal causes
  3. Strict partogram use

General Management

  1. Assess maternal condition
  2. IV fluid correction
  3. Broad-spectrum antibiotics
  4. Catheterization
  5. Correction of acidosis

Obstetric Management

🚨 Emergency
Cesarean section with active management of third stage (for fear of PPH). Exploration of birth canal post-CS for cervical tears, vaginal lacerations, vesicovaginal fistula.

Complications

🤰 Maternal
  • Postpartum hemorrhage
  • Rupture uterus
  • Sepsis
  • Fistula (vesicovaginal)
👶 Fetal
  • Neonatal sepsis
  • Convulsions (fits)
  • Severe asphyxia
  • Death
💎 Vesicovaginal Fistula
Pressure necrosis of the bladder base against the pubic symphysis → ischemic sloughing 5–7 days postpartum → continuous urinary incontinence. Almost entirely preventable by timely CS.

3. Contracted Pelvis

Female Pelvis Anatomy — Wikimedia Commons (CC)

Definitions

🟣 Anatomical

"A pelvis in which one or more of its diameters is reduced below normal by one or more centimeters."

🌸 Obstetric (the one that matters)

"A pelvis that is abnormally small in one or more of its principal diameters that interferes with normal childbirth and the normal mechanism of labor."

Etiology

A. Congenital (4 forms)

🦴 Naegele's Pelvis

Asymmetric / obliquely contracted — absence of one sacral ala.

🦴 Robert's Pelvis

Transversely contracted — absence of 2 sacral alae (double Naegele).

🦴 High Assimilation

Sacrum has 6 fused segments (5th lumbar absorbed).

🦴 Low Assimilation

Sacrum has 4 fused segments (1st sacral absorbed into lumbar).

B. Acquired

CategoryCauses
Pelvic bonesFractures, tumors, rickets, osteomalacia
Vertebral columnKyphosis, scoliosis, spondylolisthesis
Lower limbMissing/shortening, fractures, TB osteomyelitis

Classification by Level

MidpelvicInterspinous + posterior sagittal < 13.5 cmMost common
OutletBituberous < 8 cm + narrow subpubic angle1% (rarest)

CPD Tests

✋ Pinard Method (Abdominal)

Grasp head with left hand, two fingers on symphysis pubis, push head down and backward. No overlap = no disproportion; 0.5 cm overlap = moderate; overhangs = severe.

🤚 Muller-Kerr Method (Bimanual)

Left hand pushes head, right hand does vaginal exam with thumb on symphysis. More valuable method than Pinard.

Trial of Labor

📋 Definition
"It is the conduction of spontaneous labor of moderate degree of disproportion hoping for a vaginal delivery." A test of power, passage, and passenger.

Conditions: (1) Fetal + maternal monitoring, (2) No oral feeding, IV drip, (3) Adequate analgesic, (4) Augmentation by Pitocin.

Management by Degree

  • Minor degree: Vaginal delivery recommended
  • Moderate degree: Trial of labor; if failed → CS
  • Severe/extreme: Cesarean section required
🚨 Danger
"Extreme disproportion whether the fetus is living or dead" — CS is mandated regardless of fetal viability when disproportion is extreme.

4. Third Stage Complications

Pregnancy Anatomy — Wikimedia Commons (CC)

Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH)

⚠️ Definition
Blood loss following birth exceeding 500 ml (vaginal) or 1000 ml (cesarean) with manifestation of hypovolemia.

Types

Primary PPH (Early)

Within 24 hours after childbirth. More common.

Secondary PPH (Late)

After 24 hours until 6 weeks (end of puerperium).

The "4 T's" of PPH

T₁
Tone (Atony)
Most common ~70-80%
T₂
Trauma
Lacerations
T₃
Tissue
Retained placenta
T₄
Thrombin
Coagulopathies/DIC

Management of Atonic PPH

  1. Resuscitation: IV fluids, blood products rapidly
  2. Uterine massage: Bimanual compression (one hand vagina, one hand abdomen)
  3. Aortic compression: Closed fist against vertebrae superior to sacral promontory
  4. Uterotonic agents:
    • Oxytocin 10-40 IU IM/IV (first-line)
    • Ergometrine 0.2-0.4 mg IM (contraindicated in hypertension)
    • Carboprost 0.25 mg IM (may repeat q15min, max 2mg)
    • Misoprostol 200-1000 mcg SL/oral/vaginal
  5. Surgical: B-Lynch suture, uterine/ovarian/internal iliac artery ligation, embolization, hysterectomy
  6. Balloon tamponade: Sengstaken-Blakemore or Bakri balloon
🚨 Contraindication Alert
Hypertension is the unifying contraindication for ergot alkaloids AND carboprost — both raise BP. In preeclamptic PPH, use oxytocin and misoprostol only.

Uterine Inversion

⚠️ Definition
Collapse of the fundus into the uterine cavity — uterus turned inside out. Incidence: 1-2 per 1000 deliveries.

Degrees

DegreeNameDescription
1stIncompleteFundus depressed within endometrial cavity
2ndCompleteFundus protrudes through cervical os
3rdProlapsedFundus protrudes to or beyond introitus

Treatment

  1. Resuscitation: IV fluids, blood, stop cord traction
  2. Manual replacement: Push fundus up along vaginal axis with tocolytics for relaxation. Do NOT remove placenta first!
  3. Hydrostatic reduction (O'Sullivan): Warmed sterile saline infused into vagina
  4. Surgical reduction: Posterior/anterior cervix incision (last resort)

Retained Placenta

📋 Definition
Placenta fails to separate within 30 minutes after delivery. Second leading cause of PPH.

Etiology

CategoryCauses
Separated & retainedUterine atony, contraction ring
Non-separated & retainedSimple adhesion, abnormal adhesion (accreta/increta/percreta)
Escape into peritoneumComplete uterine rupture

Postpartum Collapse

⚠️ Definition
Acute event involving cardiorespiratory systems and/or brain, resulting in reduced/absent conscious level, at any stage in pregnancy and up to 6 weeks postpartum.

8 Causes

Anesthetic complication, Myocardial infarction, Pulmonary embolism, Amniotic fluid embolism, Rupture uterus, Uterine inversion, Postpartum eclampsia, Diabetic ketoacidosis.

💡 5-Minute Rule
CPR then treatment of the cause. If no response within 5 minutes of maternal collapse during pregnancy → emergency perimortem cesarean section. Emptying uterus restores venous return.

Complications of PPH

  • Early: 20% blood volume loss → tachycardia, narrowed pulse pressure, ischemic organ injury
  • Late: Sheehan syndrome (postpartum hypopituitarism from pituitary infarction during massive hemorrhage)

5. Amniotic Fluid Embolism

Pulmonary Embolism Diagram — Wikimedia Commons (CC)

Definition

⚠️ Key Definition
It is a condition in which fetal fluid, tissue, or debris enters the maternal circulation via the placental bed and causes acute anaphylaxis. Also called "anaphylactoid syndrome of pregnancy." NOT a true embolism — dominant mechanism is anaphylaxis-like reaction.
1-2
per 40,000 deliveries
5-15%
of maternal deaths
>50%
immediate death rate

How AF Enters Maternal Circulation

  1. Increased intrauterine pressure — abruption, oxytocin overdose
  2. Opened uterine/endocervical veins — genital tract lacerations, rupture uterus
🚨 Meconium Potentiator
Fetal distress → meconium-stained AF → potentiates toxic nature of amniotic fluid and worsens the condition.

Pathophysiology Cascade

  1. Entry of AF into maternal veins via placental bed
  2. Acute anaphylactoid reaction — mast-cell degranulation, complement activation
  3. Pulmonary vascular obstruction — particulate matter (squames, lanugo, vernix) lodges in pulmonary arterioles
  4. Acute cor pulmonale — right heart failure, left heart under-filled
  5. Abrupt hypoxia & cardiogenic shock — V/Q mismatch, ↓ cardiac output
  6. DIC — tissue factor from AF triggers systemic coagulation
  7. Deep coma → death — hypoxic brain injury, multi-organ failure

Classic AFE Triad

H
Hypoxia / Respiratory Distress
Dyspnea, cyanosis, pulmonary edema
C
Cardiovascular Collapse
Hypotension, cor pulmonale, arrest
D
DIC / Coagulopathy
Bleeding from all sites, low fibrinogen
💡 Diagnostic Rule
"It is suspected in any case of sudden postpartum collapse and DIC." — AFE is a clinical diagnosis of exclusion with no confirmatory test in real time.

Risk Factors (9)

Induced labor, Advanced maternal age, Multiparity, Uterine rupture, Abdominal trauma, Placental abruption, Diabetes, Cervical lacerations, Operative delivery.

⚠️ Source Caveat
AFE can occur in any pregnancy — most cases occur with NO identifiable risk factor. A "low-risk" patient is not protected.

Management (3 Pillars)

1
Cardiopulmonary Support
ICU · CPR · inotropes · ventilation
2
DIC Management
FFP · cryoprecipitate · platelets
3
Emergent Delivery
For fetal & maternal benefit

Additional: Corticosteroids (anaphylactoid component), Heparin (controversial), Multi-organ monitoring.

6. Normal & Abnormal Puerperium

Uterine Involution During Puerperium — Wikimedia Commons (CC)

Definition

📋 Key Definition
Puerperium is the time from delivery till 6 weeks (42 days) postpartum, during which most changes of pregnancy, labor, and delivery have resolved and the body has reverted to the non-pregnant state.

Genital Tract Changes

Uterus — Involution Timeline

After 1 week500 gmDescending ~1-2 cm/day
After 2 weeks250 gmNot palpable abdominally
By end of 6 weeks60-80 gmNon-pregnant size

Other Organs

VaginaRugae reappear (faster in non-lactating)Gradual
Vulva/PerineumSwelling and engorgement gone1-2 weeks
OvaryReturn of ovulation1.5-2 mo (non-lactating); 6 mo (lactating)

General Body Changes

SystemExpected Change
PulseNormal. Tachycardia (>100 b/m) = infection or anemia.
TemperatureMay rise <38°C in first 24 hrs (muscular effort). Should be <38°C lasting <24 hrs.
BreastsLarger, firmer. Colostrum for first 2-3 days. True milk day 3-5.

Abdominal wallStriae become white (albicans).
Urinary tractIncreased diuresis after delivery.
GITConstipation from atony, abdominal laxity, decreased fluid intake.
BloodIncreased thrombosis liability in first 2 weeks.
🚨 Danger
Postpartum thromboembolism window: Pregnancy hypercoagulable state persists ~2 weeks postpartum. Combine with immobility and dehydration → highest-risk window for DVT/PE.

Management of Normal Puerperium (11 Pillars)

  1. Vital signs — frequent observation after delivery
  2. Uterine massage — every 15 min for at least 1 hour
  3. Semi-sitting position — encourages lochia drainage
  4. Ambulation — minimize DVT risk
  5. Diet — 2 hrs after uncomplicated vaginal delivery, no restriction
  6. Perineal care — antiseptic wash + local antibiotic if stitches
  7. Breast care — wash nipple/areola with warm water and soap before feeds
  8. Bowel care — green vegetables and fruits to avoid constipation
  9. Newborn care — anti-D within 72 hrs if Rh–ve mother / Rh+ve baby
  10. Pelvic floor exercise — alternating contraction and relaxation
  11. Future contraception — counsel before discharge

Abnormal Puerperium

Subinvolution

⚠️ Definition
The uterus did not regress to its pre-pregnancy size by the end of the puerperium.

Causes: (1) Retained placental fragments, (2) Infection, (3) Antepartum overdistension, (4) Non-lactating women.

Treatment: Treatment of the cause + ecbolics and antibiotics.

Puerperal Pyrexia

⚠️ Definition
A rise of temperature reaching ≥38°C and lasting ≥24 hours during the first 10 days of puerperium.

7 Causes:

2Puerperal sepsisMost serious

"Any case of puerperal pyrexia should be considered puerperal infection (sepsis) until proved otherwise."

7. Puerperal Sepsis

Streptococcus pyogenes (Group A) — Wikimedia Commons (CC)

Definition

⚠️ Key Definition
It is a genital tract infection resulted from bacterial invasion during or after labor or abortion.

Causative Organisms

🟢 Aerobic Organisms

Gram +ve: Hemolytic streptococcus group A (severe), Non-hemolytic streptococci, S. aureus

Gram –ve: E. coli, Pseudomonas, Klebsiella

🔴 Anaerobic Organisms

Gram +ve: Anaerobic streptococci (the commonest)

Gram –ve: Cl. Welchii, Bacteroids

💡 Memory Anchor
Remember A-G-A: Aerobic Gram+ / Gram– / Anaerobic Gram+ (commonest) + Gram– (Cl. Welchii, Bacteroids). Anaerobic streptococci = single most common organism.

Mode of Infection

Endogenous Origin

Anaerobic streptococci are normal non-pathogenic commensals that become pathogenic in presence of devitalized tissues.

Exogenous Origin

From infected attendants, dust, instruments, etc.

Predisposing Factors

General

Bad general condition: anemia, diabetes, debilitating diseases.

Local (7 Factors)

  1. Premature rupture of membranes
  2. Prolonged labor
  3. Instrumental delivery
  4. Lacerations
  5. Marked blood loss
  6. Retained products of conception
  7. Improper aseptic techniques

Sites of Infection & Pathology

I. Primary Sites
  1. Lacerations of vulva, vagina or cervix
  2. Uterus: placental site, retained tissue/membranes
II. Secondary Sites
  1. Pelvic spread: Acute PID, parametritis, pelvic abscess
  2. Blood spread: bacteremia → septicemia → septic shock → multi-organ failure

Diagnosis

A. Symptoms (48-72 hrs postpartum)

  • Fever and rigors
  • Abdominal pain and tenderness
  • Offensive vaginal discharge
  • Symptoms of complications

B. Examination

  • General: Temperature >38°C for 24 hours
  • Abdominal: Subinvoluted tender uterus
  • Local: Offensive vaginal discharge, secondary PPH
  • Speculum: Inspect cervix and vagina

Complications

Early
  • Spread to secondary sites (pelvic abscess)
  • Complications of septicemia/pyemia
Remote
  • Infertility
  • Ectopic pregnancy
  • Asherman's syndrome
  • Sheehan's syndrome

Investigations

  • Blood picture: Leucocytosis
  • Urine analysis & culture: Midstream or catheter specimen
  • Swab & culture: Cervix/upper vagina for aerobic AND anaerobic cultures
  • Blood culture
  • U/S: Retained parts and pelvic abscess

Prevention (3 Phases)

PhaseMeasures
AntenatalProper diet, vitamins, minerals. Treat anemia, diabetes, local/distant infection.
IntranatalStrict asepsis. Minimize vaginal exams. Avoid excessive blood loss. Suture lacerations immediately. Prophylactic antibiotics in PROM/prolonged/instrumental delivery.
PostnatalMaintain aseptic precautions. Perineal/abdominal wound care. Early isolation of sepsis cases.

Treatment

  1. Isolation — separate room or fever hospital
  2. Diet — light, rich in vitamins/minerals, plenty of fluids
  3. Supportive — fluid/electrolyte correction, correct anemia
  4. Symptomatic — analgesics, antipyretics, cold fomentations
  5. Observations — pulse, temp, BP, bleeding, lochia, DVT
  6. Antibiotics:
    • Ampicillin/cephalosporin + gentamicin + metronidazole
    • or Clindamycin + gentamicin
    • Antitoxin serum for Cl. welchii
  7. Promote drainage: Fowler's position, remove stitches if purulent, misoprostol/ergometrine for lochia
  8. Incision & drainage: Pelvic abscess → posterior colpotomy + drain; Parametric abscess → incision + drain at pointing point
  9. Treat complications: DIC, renal failure, thrombophlebitis (anticoagulants)
🚨 Danger
Failure to drain a pelvic/parametric abscess leads to ongoing sepsis despite antibiotics. Drainage is definitive — antibiotics alone are insufficient for abscess.

8. Fetal Birth Injuries

Erb's Palsy (Brachial Plexus Injury) — Wikimedia Commons (CC)

Definition

📋 Key Definition
Birth trauma: Marked compression or sudden compression-decompression during delivery.

Causes — The 3 P's + Iatrogenic

MechanismCauses
PassagesContracted pelvis, soft tissue obstruction
PassengerFetal macrosomia, malpresentation, malposition
PowerPrecipitate labor
IatrogenicVentouse, forceps, internal podalic version, breech extraction

Types of Birth Injuries

A. Fractures

Hierarchy of frequency:

  1. Clavicle (most frequent)
  2. Humerus
  3. Femur
  4. Skull bones (most serious — underlying hemorrhage must be considered)

Most neonatal fractures heal with conservative treatment. Clavicle: mild analgesia. Long bones: simple splinting.

B. Cephalohematoma vs Caput Succedaneum

FeatureCephalohematomaCaput Succedaneum
OnsetHours/days after birthPresent at birth
NatureLocalized hematoma, limited by suturesDiffuse tissue oedema over >1 bone
EdgesWell-definedIll-defined
ConsistencyElastic (does not pit)Soft (pits on pressure)
ResolutionFew weeks1-2 days
⚠️ Rule Out Associated Injury
Skull fracture or intracranial bleeding should always be ruled out with cephalohematoma.

C. Nerve Palsies

💪 Erb's Palsy (Most Common)
  • Damage to brachial plexus (C5, C6, C7)
  • Traction on neck and shoulders during difficult delivery
  • Result: flaccid arm held in pronated and internally rotated position
  • Management: Physiotherapy; surgical nerve repair available
😀 Facial Palsy
  • Caused by obstetric maneuvers (forceps pressure)
  • Usually lower motor neuron injury
  • Prognosis: Excellent
  • If upper motor neuron suspected → investigate for cerebral injury

D. Sternocleidomastoid Tumor

  • Bleeding into sternocleidomastoid muscle
  • Not recognized at birth; obvious at a few weeks of age
  • Management: Physiotherapy to prevent contracture
  • Prognosis: Good

E. Lacerations

  • Usually during cesarean section
  • Usually superficial, heal without problems
  • Deeper wounds may require suturing
